# Literature Review and Reading List

This file collects the papers most relevant to FrameUtility's current question:

> **When does adding a geometrically valid but degraded view become worse than excluding it from multi-view 3D reconstruction?**

The summaries below paraphrase the papers rather than reproducing their abstracts verbatim.

## Reading order

1. RealX3D — closest degradation benchmark and the main novelty threat.
2. Emergent Outlier View Rejection in VGGT — closest feed-forward bad-view robustness paper.
3. G-MASt3R-SfM — closest MASt3R view-pruning paper.
4. VGGT — understand the main feed-forward backend.
5. MASt3R — understand the second backend and matching representation.
6. NeRF Director — broader view-selection literature.
7. Rashidi et al. 2013 — classical blur-aware frame filtering for reconstruction.
8. Zhao et al. 2016 — blur-aware visual odometry/keyframe selection.
9. BAD-NeRF — blur-aware reconstruction and physical blur modeling.
10. GaussGuard — leave-one-out view intervention conceptually close to marginal utility.
11. Structure from Motion Blur in Low Light — foundational camera-motion/blur trade-off.
12. Oba et al. 2025 — recent direct blur-removal frame selection for SfM-MVS.

---

# 1. RealX3D: A Physically-Degraded 3D Benchmark for Multi-view Visual Restoration and Reconstruction

**Authors:** Shuhong Liu et al.  
**Venue:** International Journal of Computer Vision, 2026  
**DOI:** `10.1007/s11263-026-02963-4`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11263-026-02963-4

## Abstract (paraphrased)

RealX3D argues that modern 3D reconstruction is usually evaluated on clean imagery even though practical data contain illumination changes, scattering, occlusions, reflections, defocus, and motion blur. The authors build a physically controlled benchmark with aligned low-quality/reference views and dense laser-scanned geometry, then evaluate both restoration and reconstruction systems under several degradation families.

## Methodology

- Uses a repeatable camera acquisition system and dense laser scanning.
- Captures or synthesizes controlled degradations while maintaining aligned reference observations.
- Includes motion blur, defocus blur, low light, exposure variation, smoke, dynamic occlusion, and reflection.
- Motion blur is generated by rendering intermediate poses along a camera path and integrating them over the exposure.
- Evaluates photometric quality, camera pose, depth/point cloud, and surface reconstruction.

## Main findings / conclusion

Existing 3D methods have large robustness gaps under realistic degradations. Blur causes particularly strong degradation in pose estimation, while view-inconsistent effects such as dynamic occlusions and reflections can severely corrupt geometry aggregation. Specialized deblurring/reconstruction methods do not uniformly outperform vanilla baselines across the benchmark.

## Overall summary

This is the most important paper for FrameUtility because it already establishes that realistic degradations harm modern multi-view reconstruction and provides a strong benchmark methodology. FrameUtility must therefore avoid claiming novelty for “testing blur robustness.”

## Relevance to our gap

RealX3D primarily compares **absolute performance under degraded inputs**. FrameUtility instead performs a controlled **add-vs-drop intervention on one view**:

```text
E(K_-i ∪ {Fi(b)})  vs  E(K_-i)
```

and asks when the additional observation's marginal utility becomes negative.

# 2. Emergent Outlier View Rejection in Visual Geometry Grounded Transformers

**Authors:** Jisang Han et al.  
**Venue:** CVPR 2026  
**arXiv DOI:** `10.48550/arXiv.2512.04012`  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04012

## Abstract (paraphrased)

The paper studies feed-forward 3D reconstruction when the image set includes irrelevant or low-overlap distractor views. It finds that VGGT develops internal representations that naturally separate such outliers despite not being explicitly trained with an outlier-rejection objective.

## Methodology

- Injects varying proportions of synthetic distractor images into multi-view input sets.
- Probes internal VGGT layers/attention/features.
- Identifies a layer where outlier suppression emerges.
- Derives a training-free filtering mechanism from internal signals.
- Evaluates on controlled and in-the-wild image collections.

## Main findings / conclusion

VGGT is not equally vulnerable to all bad views: it can internally distinguish many hard distractors and its own features can be used to reject them without fine-tuning.

## Overall summary

This paper establishes that **bad-view handling is already an active research problem in feed-forward reconstruction**. It also warns us that the backend may internally suppress some added observations, so FrameUtility must measure what the model actually does rather than assume every added image influences the output equally.

## Relevance to our gap

Their outliers are mainly **irrelevant/non-overlapping views**. FrameUtility studies a harder soft corruption: the scene and viewpoint remain valid, but the image evidence is progressively degraded.

# 3. G-MASt3R-SfM: Graph-based View Pruning and Multi-stage Optimization for Robust SfM

**Authors:** Toshiki Watanabe, Shintaro Ito, Natsuki Takama, Koichi Ito, Takafumi Aoki  
**Status:** arXiv preprint, 2026  
**DOI:** `10.48550/arXiv.2606.22856`  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22856

## Abstract (paraphrased)

MASt3R provides strong dense correspondence but can still create incorrect matches between non-overlapping image pairs. When these unreliable relations enter SfM optimization, camera-pose estimation can degrade substantially. The paper proposes pruning and staged optimization to suppress those outliers.

## Methodology

- Constructs a scene graph from MASt3R matching confidence.
- Uses Graph-based View Pruning to remove geometrically inconsistent/outlier views.
- Uses Multi-Stage Optimization to refine camera parameters from local to global consistency.
- Evaluates pose and 3D reconstruction on ETH3D.

## Main findings / conclusion

Explicit handling of unreliable views/matches improves robustness and accuracy over directly optimizing all MASt3R correspondences.

## Overall summary

This is the strongest MASt3R-side evidence that “more input” can be harmful when the added relations are unreliable. It makes any broad claim that harmful views are newly discovered untenable.

## Relevance to our gap

G-MASt3R-SfM focuses on **non-overlap/geometric outliers and a mitigation algorithm**. FrameUtility focuses on **continuous degradation of a valid view and measurement of its marginal utility curve**, without proposing a pruning algorithm initially.

# 4. VGGT: Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer

**Authors:** Jianyuan Wang, Minghao Chen, Nikita Karaev, Andrea Vedaldi, Christian Rupprecht, David Novotny  
**Venue:** CVPR 2025  
**DOI:** `10.1109/CVPR52734.2025.00499`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR52734.2025.00499

## Abstract (paraphrased)

VGGT is a feed-forward transformer that infers camera parameters, point maps, depth maps, and 3D point tracks directly from one to hundreds of images. It aims to unify several traditionally separate visual-geometry tasks in one network and reaches state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks.

## Methodology

- Multi-image transformer architecture with alternating/global view interactions.
- Predicts camera parameters and dense geometric outputs in one feed-forward pass.
- Trained across large-scale multi-view data and tasks.
- Evaluated on camera pose, depth, point-cloud reconstruction, and tracking.

## Main findings / conclusion

A single feed-forward model can solve several multi-view geometry tasks accurately and quickly, often without conventional geometric post-optimization.

## Overall summary

VGGT is useful for FrameUtility because the input set can be explicitly manipulated and the downstream geometry can be evaluated directly. The experiment should use fixed input sets rather than conflate VGGT-SLAM's sequential keyframe policy with the behavior of VGGT itself.

# 5. Grounding Image Matching in 3D with MASt3R

**Authors:** Vincent Leroy, Yohann Cabon, Jérôme Revaud  
**Venue:** ECCV 2024  
**DOI:** `10.1007/978-3-031-73220-1_5`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-73220-1_5

## Abstract (paraphrased)

MASt3R reframes image matching as a 3D-grounded problem by building on DUSt3R point-map regression. It augments the network with dense local features and a matching loss, and introduces efficient reciprocal matching for large dense correspondence sets.

## Methodology

- Extends DUSt3R-style point-map prediction.
- Adds a dense feature head trained for image matching.
- Introduces reciprocal matching with strong computational savings.
- Tests on difficult correspondence/localization benchmarks.

## Main findings / conclusion

3D-grounded matching can remain robust under large viewpoint changes while improving matching accuracy and downstream localization.

## Overall summary

FrameUtility needs this paper to understand what information MASt3R provides and how blur could perturb dense correspondences or confidence. The study should not treat MASt3R's matching score as a direct measure of reconstruction utility.

# 6. NeRF Director: Revisiting View Selection in Neural Volume Rendering

**Authors:** Wenhui Xiao et al.  
**Venue:** CVPR 2024  
**DOI:** `10.1109/CVPR52733.2024.01960`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR52733.2024.01960

## Abstract (paraphrased)

The paper argues that which training views are provided to a neural renderer can substantially change performance, yet view selection is often ignored. It proposes a unified evaluation framework and shows that uniform view coverage can outperform more complicated selection rules while using fewer images.

## Methodology

- Benchmarks several view-selection strategies.
- Tests the effect of training-view selection under different test-view arrangements.
- Compares random, error/uncertainty-guided, and coverage-driven selection.
- Evaluates synthetic and realistic datasets.

## Main findings / conclusion

View composition matters, and fewer but better distributed views can produce high-quality results faster than indiscriminate use of more observations.

## Overall summary

This is important background for the statement that observation value is conditional on the rest of the view set. It also reinforces that FrameUtility should not call marginal utility an intrinsic frame property.

# 7. Optimized Selection of Key Frames for Monocular Videogrammetric Surveying of Civil Infrastructure

**Authors:** Abbas Rashidi, Fei Dai, Ioannis Brilakis, Patricio Vela  
**Venue:** Advanced Engineering Informatics, 2013  
**DOI:** `10.1016/j.aei.2013.01.002`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aei.2013.01.002

## Abstract (paraphrased)

Video-based 3D reconstruction contains many redundant, noisy, and motion-blurred frames. The paper proposes automatically filtering poor-quality frames first and then selecting an informative subset for efficient, accurate videogrammetric reconstruction.

## Methodology

- Measures frame quality and removes blurred frames using a threshold.
- Selects a reduced set of informative keyframes from the remaining video.
- Runs a conventional videogrammetric/SfM pipeline.
- Compares reconstruction quality and computational cost.

## Main findings / conclusion

Filtering blurred frames and reducing redundancy can improve 3D reconstruction while keeping the number of processed frames small.

## Overall summary

This paper makes it clear that “blurred frames should sometimes be removed” is old. FrameUtility's contribution must therefore be the quantitative **marginal-utility transition**, not blur filtering itself.

# 8. Filtering Based Adaptive Visual Odometry Sensor Framework Robust to Blurred Images

**Authors:** Haiying Zhao, Yong Liu, Xiaojia Xie, Yiyi Liao, Xixi Liu  
**Venue:** Sensors, 2016  
**DOI:** `10.3390/s16071040`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/s16071040

## Abstract (paraphrased)

The authors address visual odometry under blurred input. They estimate blur severity from image-gradient statistics, classify frames as clear or blurred, and modify keyframe selection so severely blurred frames are less likely to enter VO estimation.

## Methodology

- Small Image Gradient Distribution (SIGD) blur measure.
- Adaptive blurred-image classification.
- Anti-blur keyframe-selection module attached to a VO pipeline.
- Simulation and real-data comparisons.

## Main findings / conclusion

Avoiding heavily blurred frames improves VO accuracy with modest extra computation, without requiring explicit deblurring.

## Overall summary

This is another strong prior for quality-aware frame rejection. It is methodologically different from FrameUtility because it classifies/filter frames rather than measuring continuous add-vs-drop utility.

# 9. BAD-NeRF: Bundle Adjusted Deblur Neural Radiance Fields

**Authors:** Peng Wang, Lingzhe Zhao, Ruijie Ma, Peidong Liu  
**Venue:** CVPR 2023  
**DOI:** `10.1109/CVPR52729.2023.00406`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR52729.2023.00406

## Abstract (paraphrased)

BAD-NeRF addresses NeRF reconstruction from motion-blurred images and inaccurate camera poses. Instead of treating blur as an arbitrary image-space corruption, it models the physical exposure process and jointly optimizes scene representation and camera motion during exposure.

## Methodology

- Represents each blurred observation as integration of latent sharp views along an exposure-time camera trajectory.
- Jointly estimates NeRF parameters and exposure-time camera motion / poses.
- Evaluates on synthetic and real blurred datasets.

## Main findings / conclusion

Explicit physical blur modeling and joint pose refinement substantially improve reconstruction under severe motion blur compared with methods that assume clean images.

## Overall summary

This paper is important for designing our blur synthesis. It supports using exposure integration rather than a generic Gaussian blur kernel.

# 10. GaussGuard: Leave-one-out Detection and Mitigation of View-Conditional Poisoning Attacks on 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Venue:** Neurocomputing, 2026  
**DOI:** `10.1016/j.neucom.2026.134830`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2026.134830

## Abstract (paraphrased)

GaussGuard studies malicious view-conditional poisoning of 3D Gaussian Splatting. It proposes a per-view leave-one-out procedure that reconstructs with and without suspect views to detect and mitigate attacks.

## Methodology

- Reconstructs alternative models while withholding individual training views.
- Measures whether suspicious view-dependent artifacts disappear under leave-one-out reconstruction.
- Uses the same intervention for both detection and mitigation.

## Main findings / conclusion

A harmful individual view can be diagnosed by comparing reconstruction behavior with that view present versus absent.

## Overall summary

This is conceptually close to FrameUtility because it demonstrates a leave-one-out **view intervention**. We should not claim that leave-one-out contribution itself is novel. Our distinction is natural/controlled degradation, continuous severity, and zero-crossing marginal utility rather than adversarial poisoning.

# 11. Structure from Motion Blur in Low Light

**Authors:** Yali Zheng, Shohei Nobuhara, Yaser Sheikh  
**Venue:** CVPR 2011  
**DOI:** `10.1109/CVPR.2011.5995594`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR.2011.5995594

## Abstract (paraphrased)

This work starts from the classical tension that larger camera motion can improve geometric conditioning but also creates stronger motion blur, especially under long exposure. Rather than rejecting blur, it extracts 3D and motion information encoded in blur traces of point light sources.

## Methodology

- Detects and traces motion-blurred point-light trajectories.
- Infers start/end correspondences.
- Estimates epipolar geometry from the traces.
- Recovers 3D structure and camera motion and refines them with bundle adjustment.

## Main findings / conclusion

Motion blur is not only degradation; under a suitable image-formation model it also contains information about motion and structure.

## Overall summary

This paper is useful for the motivation: geometry and blur are naturally coupled through camera motion. It also cautions against treating blur severity as synonymous with information loss in every possible algorithm.

# 12. A Study on 3D Model Reconstruction from Moving Images Considering Blur

**Authors:** Haruto Oba, Munetoshi Iwakiri, Kiyoshi Tanaka  
**Venue:** Reports of the Technical Conference of the Institute of Image Electronics Engineers of Japan, 2025  
**DOI:** `10.11371/wiieej.24.03.0_67`  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.11371/wiieej.24.03.0_67

## Abstract (paraphrased)

The paper studies SfM-MVS reconstruction from video and points out that common frame-selection approaches based on optical flow or perceptual hashing do not directly account for the image quality of each frame. It proposes removing blurred/shaky frames and evaluates the effect on reconstruction.

## Methodology

- Detects low-quality blurred/shaky video frames using image sharpness information including a Laplacian-based measure.
- Removes those frames before SfM-MVS processing.
- Compares reconstruction results against selection methods that do not explicitly account for blur.

## Main findings / conclusion

Image-quality-aware filtering can prevent poor video frames from degrading point-cloud reconstruction.

## Overall summary

This is a recent direct precedent for blur-aware frame rejection in SfM-MVS. It further narrows FrameUtility's defensible novelty to controlled marginal-utility measurement rather than a new frame-filtering rule.

# What the literature already establishes

The following claims are **not novel**:

1. Motion blur can reduce pose/reconstruction accuracy.
2. Removing blurred or low-quality frames can improve classical SfM/VO.
3. More views are not always better if some views are outliers or geometrically inconsistent.
4. Feed-forward reconstruction models have identifiable failure modes under bad views.
5. Leave-one-out view interventions have precedent.
6. View selection is context-dependent and has a long history in SfM and neural rendering.

# Remaining research gap for FrameUtility

The most defensible gap is:

> **Systematically characterize the conditional marginal utility of one geometrically valid observation as its degradation severity is continuously increased, using a fixed drop baseline, and identify when its contribution crosses from beneficial to harmful.**

Formally:

```text
U_i(b | K_-i) = E(K_-i) - E(K_-i ∪ {Fi(b)})
```

Primary outputs:

```text
U(b)
P(U < 0 | b)
b* where U(b*) = 0
```

This gap should be treated as a hypothesis to validate, not as a guaranteed novelty claim. A final paper/proposal should state that nearby literature exists and explain the difference explicitly.
